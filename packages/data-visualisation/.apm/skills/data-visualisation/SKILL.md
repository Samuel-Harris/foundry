# Data Visualisation: Visualization Analysis and Design

A navigation layer over Tamara Munzner's book, which analyses vis use through three questions: what data is shown, why the user needs it (the task), and how it is shown (the encoding and interaction idiom) [pp.41-43]. Pick a job from the first five children; for a known dataset and question, use the routing table after them; to find an idiom or term by name, use the lookup. The chapter nodes come last [p.68] [p.171].

## Children

### Jobs and lookup

- [J1 Choose a Chart or Idiom](references/jobs/j1-choose-idiom.md) · ~1,497 words. **Use when** a user has data and a question but no chart yet: classify the dataset and the task, then pick marks, channels, arrangement, colour and any reduction, ending at an idiom card [pp.45-46] [p.68] [p.171]
- [J2 Write Plotting Code](references/jobs/j2-write-plotting-code.md) · ~1,496 words. **Use when** the chart type is decided and code must be written: the card with library hints for any chart, colormap or interaction (zoom, pan, brush, selection), then the channel, baseline, ordering and colour checks [pp.126-128] [pp.250-260] [pp.276-280]
- [J3 Critique a Visualisation](references/jobs/j3-critique.md) · ~1,494 words. **Use when** reviewing or improving an existing chart, dashboard or tool: name the level at which it fails, judge whether its validation evidence (a study, a survey, adoption) is enough, and check rules of thumb such as unjustified 3D, channels, colour pitfalls and overplotting [pp.99-106] [pp.141-142] [p.332]
- [J4 Design Interactive and Multi-View Dashboards](references/jobs/j4-dashboards.md) · ~1,492 words. **Use when** a design needs several linked views, overlaid lines versus small multiples, interaction, filtering or aggregation for large data, or focus+context and elision, as in an exploratory tool or dashboard [p.269] [pp.290-292] [pp.315-317] [pp.349-350]
- [Idiom and Concept Lookup](references/lookup.md) · ~3,032 words. **Use when** you already know a name (an idiom such as "treemap" or "SPLOM", a term such as "popout", "separability" or "nested model", or a section number) and need the file that holds it; it maps every chapter, section and idiom card of the contents [pp.6-15]

## Disambiguation

- **J1 Choose a Chart or Idiom vs J2 Write Plotting Code.** J1 decides which idiom fits the data and task; J2 starts from a decided idiom and gathers the encoding rules the code must honour. A request like "plot this" with no chart named goes to J1 first [p.68] [pp.124-128].
- **J3 Critique a Visualisation vs 6 Rules of Thumb vs 4 Analysis: Four Levels for Validation.** J3 is the ordered critique route across chapters 4, 5, 6, 7, 10 and 14; go to chapter 6 or 4 directly only when the question is about one rule or about validation methods themselves [pp.99-106] [pp.141-142].
- **J4 Design Interactive and Multi-View Dashboards vs 11 Manipulate View vs 12 Facet into Multiple Views.** J4 routes across chapter 6 and chapters 11 to 15 for a whole tool; chapter 11 is change within one view and chapter 12 is splitting across views or layers [p.269] [p.290].
- **Idiom and Concept Lookup vs 2 What: Data Abstraction vs 3 Why: Task Abstraction.** The lookup finds a file by name; chapters 2 and 3 are for when you know the data and question but not the name of the idiom [pp.45-46] [p.68].
- **7 Arrange Tables vs 13 Reduce Items and Attributes.** Chapter 7 lays out every item; chapter 13 applies when items or attributes outnumber what a layout can show legibly [p.171] [pp.324-325].
