# 1.5 Why Depend on Vision?

## Principle

<!-- verbatim p.31 -->
> Visualization, as the name implies, is based on exploiting the human visual system as a means of communication.
<!-- /verbatim -->

The book focuses exclusively on vision rather than other senses because it is both well characterized and suitable for transmitting information [p.31].

## Vision

The visual system is a very high-bandwidth channel to the brain, and much visual processing happens in parallel at the preconscious level [p.31]. Visual popout is the example: one red item is noticed immediately among grey ones, whether the field of other objects is large or small, because processing runs in parallel across the whole visual field. Vision also feeds higher-level processes that involve conscious control of attention [pp.31-32].

## Other senses

- **Sound** is poorly suited to overviews of large information spaces. Background visual processing lets us act as if we see a huge amount at once, though only a tiny part of the visual field is seen in high resolution at any instant; sound is experienced as a sequential stream, not merged into a simultaneous experience [p.32]. The book says this difference "may explain" why sonification has never taken off despite many attempts [p.32].
- **Taste and smell** are ruled out by technology: there is no viable recording and reproduction technology for them yet [p.32].
- **Touch and kinesthetic** channels have haptic devices, but these cover only a very limited part of what we can sense, and exploring their effectiveness for abstract information is at a very early stage [p.32].
