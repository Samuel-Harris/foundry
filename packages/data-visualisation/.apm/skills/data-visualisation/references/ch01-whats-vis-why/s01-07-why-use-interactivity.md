# 1.7 Why Use Interactivity?

## Principle

<!-- verbatim p.34 -->
> Interactivity is crucial for building vis tools that handle complexity.
<!-- /verbatim -->

## Why static views fall short

When datasets are large enough, the limits of people and of displays preclude showing everything at once; interaction, where user actions change the view, is the way forward [p.34]. A single static view shows only one aspect of a dataset. Some combinations of simple datasets and tasks need only one visual encoding, but an interactively changing display supports many possible queries [p.34].

## What interaction enables

- Investigation at multiple levels of detail, from a high-level overview through several levels of summarization down to a fully detailed view of a small part [p.34].
- Different ways of representing and summarizing the data, presented so as to support understanding the connections between the alternatives [p.34].

Before fast computer graphics, visualization was limited to static images on paper; computer-based vis makes interactivity possible and vastly increases the scope of vis tools. Static representations are within the book's scope, but interaction is an intrinsic part of many idioms [p.34].
