# 3 Why: Task Abstraction

The "why" part of the what–why–how framework: a small vocabulary for describing why someone uses a vis, built from actions (verbs) and targets (nouns) [pp.68-69] [p.80]. Actions sit at three independent levels: analyze (consume by discovering, presenting or enjoying, or produce by annotating, recording or deriving), search (lookup, locate, browse, explore, by whether target identity and location are known) and query (identify one target, compare some, summarize all) [p.68] [p.70]. Targets are trends, outliers and features for all data; one value, extremes or the distribution of one attribute; dependency, correlation or similarity between attributes; topology or paths in networks; shape in spatial data [p.68]. The book asks you to translate domain-specific task descriptions into these generic terms, to consider one goal at a time when asking how an idiom supports it, and to let the task abstraction guide the data abstraction, including whether to derive new data [p.69].

## Disambiguation

- **The Big Picture vs Actions vs Targets.** 3.1 states the whole vocabulary in one paragraph with no definitions or examples; 3.4 defines and illustrates each action and 3.5 each target [p.68] [pp.70-82].
- **Actions vs Analyzing and Deriving: Examples.** 3.4 (under Produce) defines the derive action and how to derive attributes and dataset types; 3.7 applies the framework, including derivation, to three named systems [pp.75-78] [pp.84-89].
- **Why Analyze Tasks Abstractly? vs Who: Designer or User.** 3.2 is about rewriting a task in generic terms; 3.3 is about who holds a goal or makes a design choice [pp.68-70].
- **Actions vs Targets.** Every task needs both: actions are verbs and targets are the nouns they act on [p.80].
