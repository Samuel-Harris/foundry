# 3.4.4 Query

## Three scopes

Once a search has found a target or set of targets, the low-level goal is to query them at one of three scopes: identify, compare or summarize. The progression is an increase in the number of targets considered: one, some or all [p.79].

<!-- verbatim p.79 -->
> That is, identify refers to a single target, compare refers to multiple targets, and summarize refers to the full set of possible targets.
<!-- /verbatim -->

Example: on a choropleth map of US election results with each state coloured by the winning party, a user can identify one state's result, compare one state's result with another's, or summarize across all states to count how many favoured each candidate or to see the overall distribution of margins of victory [p.79].

<a id="s03.04.04.01"></a>

### Identify

<!-- verbatim p.79 -->
> The scope of identify is a single target.
<!-- /verbatim -->

What identify returns depends on the search that preceded it [pp.79-80]:

- **After lookup or locate** (known targets), identify returns their characteristics. Example: on a static election map with states coloured red or blue and saturation showing the proportion, identify California's winning party and margin of victory [pp.79-80].
- **After browse or explore** (targets matching characteristics), identify returns specific references. Example: identify the state with the highest margin of victory [p.80].

<a id="s03.04.04.02"></a>

### Compare

<!-- verbatim p.80 -->
> The scope of compare is multiple targets. Comparison tasks are typically more difficult than identify tasks and require more sophisticated idioms to support the user.
<!-- /verbatim -->

For example, being able to inspect a single target in detail is often necessary, but not sufficient, for comparison [p.80].

<a id="s03.04.04.03"></a>

### Summarize

<!-- verbatim p.80 -->
> The scope of summarize task is all possible targets. A synonym for summarize is overview, a term is frequently used in the vis literature both as a verb, where it means to provide a comprehensive view of everything, and as a noun, where it means a summary display of everything.
<!-- /verbatim -->

The goal of providing an overview is extremely common in visualisation [p.80].
