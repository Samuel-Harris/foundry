# 4.6.5 Mismatches

<!-- verbatim p.106 -->
> A common problem in weak vis projects is a mismatch between the level at which the benefit is claimed and the validation methodologies chosen.
<!-- /verbatim -->

Two examples [p.106]:

- The benefit of a new visual encoding idiom cannot be validated by wall-clock timings of the algorithm, which address a level downstream of the claim [p.106].
- The threat of a mischaracterised task cannot be addressed by a formal laboratory study, where the study designers dictate the participants' task [p.106].

The nested model separates vis design into levels precisely to guide validation according to each level's threats [p.106].

No single research paper could address all four levels in detail; it would need hundreds of pages and might take a decade. Each paper uses a small subset of methods, chosen carefully to match the levels where it claims contributions [p.106].
