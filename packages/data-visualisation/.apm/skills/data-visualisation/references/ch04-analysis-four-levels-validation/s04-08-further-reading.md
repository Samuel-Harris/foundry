# 4.8 Further Reading

## Sources by topic

- **The big picture.** The four-level nested model was first presented as a paper [Munzner 09a], with blocks and guidelines between them discussed in a follow-up [Meyer et al. 13]; both have many more references. McGrath's analysis of the strengths and limitations of experimental methods [McGrath 94] influenced the book's partition of validation techniques by level [p.116].
- **Problem-driven work.** Design study methodology, with a nine-stage framework and advice on avoiding 32 pitfalls [Sedlmair et al. 12]; and the Multidimensional In-depth Long-term Case studies (MILC) approach, which also advocates working closely with domain users [Shneiderman and Plaisant 06] [p.117].
- **Abstraction level.** An argument that both data and task abstractions are important points of departure for designers [Pretorius and van Wijk 09]. Abstraction-level problems belong to requirements elicitation and analysis in software engineering, for which a book chapter is a good start [Maalej and Thurimella 13] [p.117].
- **Algorithm level.** Two textbooks [Telea 07, Ward et al. 10] and a large handbook [Hansen and Johnson 05]; recent surveys or specific research papers for detail; and a popular general algorithms textbook that also covers complexity analysis [Cormen et al. 90] [p.117].
- **Human–computer interaction.** A comprehensive academic textbook [Sharp et al. 07] and a very accessible book for practitioners [Kuniavsky 03] [p.117].
- **Evaluation methods.** A book chapter surveying evaluation and validation methods for vis, with extensive discussion of qualitative methods [Carpendale 08]; a discussion of evaluation challenges calling for more repositories of data and tasks [Plaisant 04]; and a viewpoint article on why, how and when to do user studies [Kosara et al. 03] [p.117].
- **Field studies.** Contextual inquiry, covered by one of its pioneers [Holtzblatt and Jones 93] [p.117].
- **Experiment design.** For lab studies: a cogent, accessible monograph [Hornbaek 13], a remarkably witty book [Field and Hole 03], and a textbook with many visualisation examples [Purchase 12] [p.118].
