# 5.5 Channel Effectiveness

Why the channel rankings of Section 5.4.2 hold, and what else limits a channel. The section answers how the rankings are justified, how much information each channel can encode, and whether channels can be used independently or interfere, by analysing channels against five criteria: accuracy, discriminability, separability, visual popout and perceptual grouping [p.128]. The rankings are primarily based on accuracy but also take the other four into account [p.131].

## Children

- [5.5.1 Accuracy](s05-05-01-accuracy.md) · ~693 words. **Use when** you need to know how precisely people judge magnitudes on a channel: Stevens' power law exponents and the Cleveland–McGill and Heer–Bostock experimental orderings (position, length, angle, area and below) [pp.128-131]
- [5.5.2 Discriminability](s05-05-02-discriminability.md) · ~311 words. **Use when** an attribute has many distinct values and you must check the channel has enough distinguishable levels (bins), as with line width, or decide to aggregate into bins or switch channel [p.131]
- [5.5.3 Separability](s05-05-03-separability.md) · ~583 words. **Use when** encoding two or more attributes on one mark and you need to know whether the paired channels (position and hue, size and hue, width and height, red and green) can be attended to separately or fuse [pp.131-134]
- [5.5.4 Popout](s05-05-04-popout.md) · ~668 words. **Use when** one item or a small set must be spotted instantly among many others, or when a search task is slow because the target differs from distractors on more than one channel [pp.134-136]
- [5.5.5 Grouping](s05-05-05-grouping.md) · ~462 words. **Use when** items should be seen as belonging together: the strength order of containment, connection, proximity and similarity cues, and the care shape and motion need [pp.136-137]

## Disambiguation

- **Accuracy vs Discriminability.** Accuracy asks how close a perceived magnitude is to the true one; discriminability asks how many distinguishable steps a channel offers and whether that covers the number of values to show [p.128] [p.131].
- **Separability vs Popout.** Both concern combining channels. Separability is whether two channels encoding two attributes can be read independently; popout is whether a single target is found immediately, which designers should count on for one channel at a time, since most pairs do not support it and only a few do [p.131] [p.135].
- **Separability vs Grouping.** Separability is about selectively attending to each of several encoded attributes; grouping is about which encodings make items perceived as a set, such as all items of one hue [p.133] [p.136].
- **Popout vs Grouping.** Popout finds one distinct item fast regardless of distractor count; grouping makes a whole category perceivable as a set by attending to its shared level [p.134] [p.136].
