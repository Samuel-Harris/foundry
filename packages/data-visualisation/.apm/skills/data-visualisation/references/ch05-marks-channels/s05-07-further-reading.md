# 5.7 Further Reading

## Sources by topic

- **The big picture.** Bertin proposed the highly influential theory of visual marks and channels in the 1960s [Bertin 67] [p.139].
- **The channel rankings.** The ranking of channel effectiveness is the author's synthesis across many previous authors and does not come directly from any specific source. Influences: measured-response experiments ranking visual channels [Cleveland and McGill 84a]; models [Cleveland 93a]; design guidelines for matching channels to data type [Mackinlay 86]; books on visualisation [Ware 13] and cartography [MacEachren 95]; and more recent work on crowdsourced judgements [Heer and Bostock 10], taxonomy-based glyph design [Maguire et al. 12] and glyph design in general [Borgo et al. 13] [pp.139-140].
- **Psychophysical measurement.** Foundational work on the variable distinguishability of visual channels, the categorisation of channels as metathetic identity and prothetic magnitude, and scales of measurement [Stevens 57, Stevens 75] [p.140].
- **Effectiveness and expressiveness principles.** Expressiveness for matching channel to data type and effectiveness for choosing channels by importance ordering appeared in a foundational paper [Mackinlay 86] [p.140].
- **Perception.** The chapter does not explain the mechanisms behind the phenomena it uses; it distils an enormous literature to the minimum a beginning designer needs, and does not cover, for example, the Gestalt principles [p.140].
- **Introductions to perception for vis.** Ware's two books [Ware 08, Ware 13] are broad, thorough and highly recommended, with more detail on nearly every topic in the chapter, including separability and popout [p.140].
- **Popout.** An overview of the literature on popout and other perceptual phenomena, with interactive demos, at <http://www.csc.ncsu.edu/faculty/healey/PP> [Healey 07]; and a core paper that begins to untangle which low-level features early visual processing detects [Treisman and Gormican 88] [p.140].
