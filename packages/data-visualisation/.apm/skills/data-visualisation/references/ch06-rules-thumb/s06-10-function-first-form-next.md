# 6.10 Function First, Form Next

## The rule

<!-- verbatim p.165 -->
> The best vis designs should shine in terms of both form and function; that is, they should be both beautiful and effective. Nevertheless, in this book, I focus on function.
<!-- /verbatim -->

## Why function first

- **Ugly but effective can be refined.** Form can be refined to make an effective design more beautiful while keeping its effectiveness, and a designer without graphic design training can collaborate with someone who has it [p.165].
- **Beautiful but ineffective must be discarded.** Such a design will probably need to be tossed out and restarted, because progressive refinement is usually not possible; so the author does not advocate "form first" [pp.165-166].
- **The underlying argument.** This mirrors the first chapter's claims about the size of the vis design space and the fact that most designs are ineffective [p.166].

## Not "form never"

<!-- verbatim p.166 -->
> Equally important is the point that I don't advocate "form never": visual beauty does indeed matter, given that vis makes use of human visual perception.
<!-- /verbatim -->

Given two equally effective systems, people prefer the beautiful one, and good visual form enhances the effectiveness of visual representations [p.166]. The book leaves graphic design principles to other sources and concentrates on vis effectiveness because of the lack of other resources on it [p.166].
