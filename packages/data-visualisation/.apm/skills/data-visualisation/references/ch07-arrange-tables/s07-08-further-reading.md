# 7.8 Further Reading

## Sources by topic

- **The big picture.** Taxonomies and frameworks that influenced the book's: the infovis design space [Card and Mackinlay 99], a tutorial on visual idioms [Keim 97], the grammar of graphics [Wilkinson 05], multidimensional multivariate vis [McGuffin 14], generalized pair plots [Emerson et al. 12], product plots [Wickham and Hofmann 11], a recent taxonomy [Heer and Shneiderman 12], and Bertin's Semiology of Graphics [Bertin 67] [p.200].
- **History.** The history of statistical graphics such as time-series line charts, bar charts, pie charts and circle charts, at <http://www.datavis.ca/milestones> [Friendly 08] [p.200].
- **Statistical graphics.** Cleveland's book on traditional charts, including bar, line and dot charts and scatterplots [Cleveland 93b] [p.200].
- **Stacked charts.** Streamgraphs were popularised by ThemeRiver [Havre et al. 00]; later work analyses their geometry and aesthetics [Byron and Wattenberg 08] [p.200].
- **Bar charts versus line charts.** Cognitive psychology guidelines for choosing between them [Zacks and Tversky 99] [p.200].
- **Banking to 45 degrees.** Aspect ratio control by banking [Cleveland et al. 88, Cleveland 93b], extended to an automatic multiscale framework [Heer and Agrawala 06] [p.201].
- **Heatmaps and matrix reordering.** Historical reviews of heatmaps, cluster heatmaps and matrix reordering [Wilkinson and Friendly 09] and of matrix reordering and seriation [Liiv 10] [p.201].
- **Parallel coordinates.** Proposed independently at the same time by a geometer [Inselberg and Dimsdale 90, Inselberg 09] and a statistician [Wegman 90] [p.201].
- **Radial layouts.** Characterised through empirical user studies [Diehl et al. 10] and surveyed [Draper et al. 09] [p.201].
- **Dense layouts.** Explored extensively for many data types [Keim 00]; SeeSoft was an early dense layout for text and source code [Eick et al. 92], and Tarantula a later one [Jones et al. 02] [p.201].
