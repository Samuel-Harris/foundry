# 8.2 Why Use Given?

## Spatial position as the substrate

<!-- verbatim p.204 -->
> The common case with spatial data is that the given spatial position is the attribute of primary importance because the central tasks revolve around understanding spatial relationships.
<!-- /verbatim -->

In these cases the right visual encoding choice is to use the provided spatial position as the substrate for the visual layout, rather than to encode other attributes with marks using the spatial position channel [pp.204-205].

## The effectiveness argument

The choice may seem obvious from common sense, but it also follows from the effectiveness principle: the most effective channel, spatial position, shows the most important aspect of the data, the spatial relationships between elements [p.205].

The margin note on this page points to Section 5.4.1 as covering "the expressiveness principle", while the body text names the effectiveness principle; the book does not reconcile the two [p.205].

## When the given layout is not primary

Datasets with spatial attribute semantics may have tasks where understanding spatial relationships is not the primary concern. Then the question of which other attributes to encode with spatial position is open again [p.205].
