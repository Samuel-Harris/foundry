# 9.6 Further Reading

## Sources by topic

- **Network layout.** An early survey of network vis [Herman et al. 00] and a later one [von Landesberger et al. 11]; a tutorial covering node–link, matrix and hybrid approaches, including node ordering [McGuffin 12]; and an analysis of edge densities that puts the readability limit at roughly four times as many edges as nodes [Melançon 06] [p.241].
- **Force-directed placement.** An algorithmically oriented overview in a book chapter [Brandes 01], and the Graph Embedder (GEM) algorithm as a sophisticated placement algorithm with a built-in termination condition [Frick et al. 95] [p.241].
- **Multilevel network layout.** sfdp [Hu 05], FM3 [Hachul and Jünger 04] and TopoLayout [Archambault et al. 07b] [p.241].
- **Matrix versus node–link views.** The design space of matrix, node–link and hybrid layouts for social network analysis [Henry and Fekete 06, Henry et al. 07], and the empirical study characterising their uses for a broad set of abstract tasks [Ghoniem et al. 05] [p.241].
- **Design space of tree drawings.** A hand-curated visual bibliography of hundreds of tree-drawing approaches at <http://treevis.net> [Schulz 11]; design guidelines for 2D tree representations from an analysis of their space efficiency [McGuffin and Robert 10]; and an analysis of tree drawing beyond node–link layouts [Schulz et al. 11] [pp.241-242].
- **Treemaps.** First proposed at the University of Maryland [Johnson and Shneiderman 91]; an empirical study gave perceptual guidelines by identifying the data densities at which length-encoded bar charts become less effective than area-encoded treemaps [Kong et al. 10] [p.242].
