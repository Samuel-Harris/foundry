# 12.6 Further Reading

## The big picture

- An extensive survey of idioms using partitioning into multiple views, superimposing layers, changing the viewpoint through navigation and embedding focus into context, with an assessment of the empirical evidence [Cockburn et al. 08] [p.320].
- A monograph on the trade-offs between these choices, with guidelines for when and how to use them [Lam and Munzner 10] [p.320].
- A paper quantifying costs and benefits of multiple views versus navigation within one view for visual comparisons at multiple scales [Plumlee and Ware 06] [p.320].
- A design space of "composite vis": juxtapose, superimpose, overload by embedding, nest one view inside another, and integrate views with explicit link marks [Javed and Elmqvist 12] [p.321].
- Approaches to comparison: juxtapose, superimpose and encode with derived data [Gleicher et al. 11] [p.321].

## Coordinating juxtaposed views

- Concise guidelines for designing multiple-view systems [Baldonado et al. 00], and a later survey of multiple-view idioms [Roberts 07] [p.321].
- The Improvise toolkit's many forms of linking [Weaver 04], and follow-on work on designing coordinated multiple-view systems [Weaver 10] [p.321].

## Partitioning

- HiVE: flexible subdivision of attribute hierarchies via interactive controls and a command language, for systematic exploration of the partitioning design space [Slingsby et al. 09], with spatially ordered treemaps as one layout [Wood and Dykes 08] [p.321].

## Glyphs

- A state of the art report on glyphs as the place to start [Borgo et al. 13], and an earlier handbook chapter on glyph design [Ward 08] [p.321].
- Macroglyph and microglyph design in Chapters 5 and 6 of Ware's textbook [Ware 13]; glyph placement [Ward 02] [p.321].
- Glyph design from a design study of biological experiment workflows [Maguire et al. 12], and experiments on visual channels in colour-enhanced star plot glyphs [Klippel et al. 09] [p.321].

## Linked highlighting

- Proposed at Bell Labs as brushing [Becker and Cleveland 87]; a chapter 20 years later follows up the ideas in depth [Wills 08] [p.321].

## Superimposing layers

- A concise, readable blog post on layer design and luminance contrast [Stone 10] [p.321].
- An article on the benefits of superimposed dot charts compared with grouped bar charts [Robbins 06] [pp.321-322].
