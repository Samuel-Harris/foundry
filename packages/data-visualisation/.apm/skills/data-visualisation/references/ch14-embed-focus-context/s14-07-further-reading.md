# 14.7 Further Reading

## Sources by topic

- **The big picture.** Two extensive surveys of idioms that change the viewpoint through navigation, partition into multiple views or embed focus into context, assessing the empirical evidence on these three approaches and giving design guidelines [Cockburn et al. 08, Lam and Munzner 10] [p.362].
- **Early work.** The Bifocal Display [Spence and Apperley 82] and polyfocal cartography [Kadmon and Shlomi 78]; an early taxonomy of distortion-based interfaces introduced the vocabulary of magnification and transformation functions [Leung and Apperley 94] [p.362].
- **Fisheye views.** Generalised fisheye views [Furnas 82, Furnas 86], followed 20 years later by a paper questioning the emphasis on geometric distortion [Furnas 06] [pp.362-363].
- **3D perspective.** The Perspective Wall [Mackinlay et al. 91] and Cone Trees [Robertson et al. 91] from Xerox PARC [p.363].
- **Frameworks.** Elastic presentation spaces [Carpendale et al. 95, Carpendale et al. 96] and nonlinear magnification fields [Keahey and Robertson 97] [p.363].
- **Hyperbolic geometry.** Hyperbolic 2D trees from Xerox PARC [Lamping et al. 95] and 3D hyperbolic networks from Stanford [Munzner 98] [p.363].
- **Stretch and squish navigation.** TreeJuxtaposer proposed guaranteed visibility and stretch and squish algorithms for large trees [Munzner et al. 03], followed by the PRISAD framework with further scalability and several data types [Slack et al. 06] [p.363].
