# Idiom and Concept Lookup

A name-to-file index for the book, built from its table of contents: every idiom card, every chapter and section, and the book's key concepts, each with the PDF pages it draws on [pp.6-15]. Use it when you know what something is called; use the router's routing table or a job node when you know only the data and the question. Section numbers follow the book [pp.6-15].

- [2 What: Data Abstraction](ch02-what-data-abstraction/node.md) · ~843 words. **Use when** classifying a dataset (table, network, tree, field, geometry), its attributes (categorical, ordinal, quantitative; sequential, diverging, cyclic) or which attributes are keys versus values [pp.45-46]

- [7 Arrange Tables](ch07-arrange-tables/node.md) · ~885 words. **Use when** the data is a table and you must choose its spatial layout by counting key and value attributes: scatterplots, bars, lines, heatmaps, SPLOMs, parallel coordinates, pies [pp.169-171]

- [12 Facet into Multiple Views](ch12-facet-into-multiple-views/node.md) · ~679 words. **Use when** information should be split across side-by-side linked views, partitioned into small multiples, or superimposed as layers in one frame [pp.289-290]
- [13 Reduce Items and Attributes](ch13-reduce-items-attributes/node.md) · ~408 words. **Use when** there are too many items or attributes to show: filter them out or aggregate them into histograms, boxplots, clusters, regions or reduced dimensions [pp.323-324]
- [14 Embed: Focus+Context](ch14-embed-focus-context/node.md) · ~1,062 words. **Use when** detail and its surrounding context must appear in one view: elision by degree of interest, lenses, fisheye or other distortion [pp.347-348]

## Disambiguation

- **Analysis Case Studies vs Arrange Tables.** Chapter 15 analyses six whole systems built from the idioms that chapters 7 to 14 define one at a time; look up an idiom in chapters 7 to 14 and a system in chapter 15 [pp.365-367].
- **Rules of Thumb vs Marks and Channels.** Both justify encoding choices; chapter 5 gives perceptual rankings for channels, chapter 6 gives slogans about whole designs such as 3D, animation and overviews [p.120] [pp.141-142].
- **Manipulate View vs Facet into Multiple Views vs Reduce Items and Attributes vs Embed: Focus+Context.** Four of the book's five answers to complexity: change one view over time, split into views or layers, show less, or keep focus and context in one view [p.269] [p.290].
